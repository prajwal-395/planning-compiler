# Ren native rendering throughput: first planning cycle

This is a real, deliberately incomplete planning cycle based on a read-only checkout of `prajwal-395/video_editing_pilot` at commit `2378f81b5eb413c8dcfe31bff4d01bb4b9814cfd` (2026-10-13). The plan models the stated objective, the current FilmCraft + EffectCraft system, existing Ren native rendering as the baseline, the four candidate strategies (caching, batching, pipeline changes, backend substitutions) as proposed alternative invoke methods for `apply-throughput-change`, output fidelity, memory and platform constraints, and CPU, GPU, memory, and Resolve-instance resources. `measure-native-baseline` invokes `ren-measurement-protocol@1` and orders before `apply-throughput-change`. No alternative carries an estimate: no recorded Ren measurement compares them on one workload, so their costs stay unknown rather than invented. Details and source pointers are below.

No Ren code or data was changed. No Ren render, benchmark, Resolve process, or profiling command was run. The cited results are records already committed in the Ren repository. Since those results use different engines, work, and render paths, none is represented as an end-to-end Ren baseline or as proof of an optimization.

## Decision sequence

`decisions.jsonl` contains three append-only PC-02 events in the order required by the planning workflow:

1. `ren-native-initial-intent-20261013`: before viewing compiler output, the planner intended to start with batching in FilmCraft + EffectCraft.
2. `ren-native-compiler-recommendation-20261013`: run against the plan with all four strategies modelled. `pc frontier` reports `0 ranked, 6 unestimated`: the four strategy proposals are tied at 0.333 and each is blocked by `measure-native-baseline`, which is listed first as the highest-scoring unestimated critical task (0.667). The unestimated list is structural order, not a ranking against estimated work. The discovery frontier names investigating the 4 alternatives for `apply-throughput-change`. The compiler did not prefer any strategy and did not generate an optimization idea.
3. `ren-native-final-decision-20261013`: the final decision drops batching-first and accepts the baseline investigation; one alternative is accepted with `pc accept` only once evidence on one workload separates them.

This is an **Investigated** decision under section 3.3: the compiler made the evidence gap actionable and changed the initial batching-first intent to measure-first, because it showed batching tied with the other three strategies and blocked by the missing baseline. It did not identify a better rendering strategy. There is no `outcomes.jsonl` because no execution or outcome was observed. The eventual outcome must be appended only after the baseline and a selected change are executed and measured.

## What existing evidence says

`evidence.jsonl` records the cited timings as `duration_minutes` observations with `basis = "absolute"`. Each workload has its own subject (for example `capability:filmcraft-4k-h264-96-frames@7bd7621`) that names nothing in the plan, so P5 holds them as pending and they never move the alternatives: they measure different engines, fixtures, and render paths. Correctness test counts and SSIM fidelity values have no IR metric and stay cited below; fidelity remains part of the acceptance criterion.

- `docs/UPSTREAM_ENGINE_INTAKE.md` records FilmCraft 0.5.0 at `7bd7621`: a separate 413-frame H.264 run at 30.30 s / 13.648 fps, and 225 frames at SSIM 0.980-0.982 against source decode. For a later 4K, 96-frame fixture, the 2026-10-10 old/new pin medians were 3.69 s / 26.0 fps and 3.98 s / 24.1 fps, with overlapping noisy repeats and near-identical output bytes. This does not establish a throughput regression or improvement across pins. Later fixed-corpus entries record `filmcraft-edit-50` at 1.07 s / 103.5 MB (old pin) and 0.772 s / 102.8 MB (new pin); those are edit-operation harness timings, not output-render throughput.
- The same intake record reports EffectCraft's 75 RGBA frames in 0.28 s and ProRes 4444 alpha in 0.60 s. These workloads are too small and different to compare against FilmCraft's H.264 export or Ren's full pipeline.
- `scripts/probe_render_control_plane.py` documents a 2026-10-02 Resolve experiment: during a 26-second, 1800-frame render, reads stayed responsive but writes did nothing; the render held the exclusive Resolve lease. The script also describes measurements of one-frame jobs, a five-job batch, and a 150-frame range, but the source file does not publish their timing results. This points to an architectural question about Resolve scheduling and per-job overhead, not a measured native-engine speedup.
- `docs/OTIO_COMPILATION_MEASURED.md` reports a real timeline-placement change from 13.35 s to 1.26 s of exclusive Resolve hold, with all 63 items field-identical in its comparison and four additional reel comparisons with zero field differences. That is evidence that offline timeline compilation can reduce Resolve control-plane work while preserving those compared fields. It does not measure FilmCraft or EffectCraft frame-render throughput.
- `library/tools/perf_ledger.py` is the documented source for per-run timing, including Resolve waits; `ren profile` reads it. A future baseline should use this ledger plus engine/render receipts to separate Resolve queue/lease time, native engine time, encoding, and pipeline work.
- `native/ren-filmcraft` and `native/ren-effectcraft` own pinned engine revisions. `docs/UPSTREAM_ENGINE_INTAKE.md` says FilmCraft's headless CLI closure contains no desktop UI crates, while EffectCraft's CLI links `effectcraft-ui-egui` and its `eframe`/`egui`/`winit`/`wgpu` dependency family. That is a concrete dependency investigation, not evidence those dependencies can be removed without breaking required rendering.

## Questions from section 10

- **Can the compiler identify a better strategy?** Not yet. With all four strategies modelled, it reports them as tied and blocked by the missing comparable baseline, which it lists as the critical open work. That was enough to overturn the batching-first intent, but not to rank caching, batching, pipeline changes, or backend substitutions by end-to-end throughput.
- **Can it find unnecessary dependencies or concurrency?** Not from this cycle. The EffectCraft headless dependency closure is worth inspecting, and the FilmCraft 2026 intake records that an earlier export change enabled GPU rendering by default. Dependency necessity, usable parallelism, memory pressure, and platform compatibility need a controlled build/profile and correctness check.
- **Can it prioritize an architecture-changing investigation?** It listed the baseline/profile task first among the unestimated work because it is critical and unblocked while every strategy waits on it. The evidence suggests measuring the boundary between Resolve orchestration and native frame rendering first; whether that warrants an architecture change remains unproven.
- **Can it predict greatest benefit?** No. Every alternative is unestimated, and existing measurements use incomparable fixtures and paths. One tool gap showed up: the frontier describes `measure-native-baseline` as `0.000 expected minutes ... rest on declared guesses` although its capability declares no estimate at all. The old/new FilmCraft 4K timing spread is noisy, while the EffectCraft and FilmCraft values describe different work. A comparable fixed workload and per-stage profile are needed before estimating relative gains.
- **Does the contribution justify planning-data maintenance?** Unknown after one small cycle. This plan, its pending evidence, and three decision events make the evidence gap and the changed decision traceable. Time spent creating and maintaining them was not instrumented, so the operational cost must be measured across later cycles.

## Next executed outcome

Run a fixed, representative Ren native-render corpus on the supported platform and record the Ren revision, engine pins, render settings, warm/cold state, repeated wall times, engine throughput, peak memory, resource contention, correctness results, and output-fidelity measurements. Use `ren profile`/the performance ledger and engine receipts to locate the dominant stage. Then record per-alternative estimates or measurements on the same corpus and constraints, accept one alternative, and measure it. Record the resulting measurements as evidence and append a PC outcome linked to `ren-native-final-decision-20261013`; do not treat estimates or this research-only cycle as an observed improvement.
